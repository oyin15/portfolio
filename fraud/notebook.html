Card Fraud Analytics & Detection — End-to-End Case Study¶

Author: Oyindamola Obisesan

Business question: A card issuer is losing money to fraud and its review team can only inspect a small share of transactions each day. Where is fraud concentrated, what changed this year, and how much loss can a model prevent at a review capacity the team can actually staff?

Data: 120,000 card transactions across 8,000 customers, Oct 2025 – Sep 2026. The dataset is synthetic, simulated to mirror real fraud patterns; no real customer data is used.

What this notebook covers

  1. Data quality checks
  2. KPI overview — fraud rate, fraud losses, trend
  3. Where fraud concentrates — merchant category, channel, time of day, account age
  4. Predictive model with a time-based train/test split
  5. Choosing a review threshold by dollars saved, not accuracy
  6. Recommendations

4. Predictive model¶

Setup choices that matter in fraud:

  • Time-based split — train on Oct 2025–Jun 2026, test on Jul–Sep 2026. A random split leaks future patterns into training and overstates performance.
  • PR-AUC as the headline metric — with ~1.5% fraud, ROC-AUC looks great almost regardless; precision-recall shows how useful the alerts really are.
  • Two models — logistic regression as an explainable baseline, gradient boosting as the challenger.

features_num = ["amount_usd", "customer_age", "account_age_days", "is_international", "new_device",
                "distance_from_home_km", "txn_count_last_24h", "hour"]
X = pd.get_dummies(df[features_num + ["merchant_category", "channel", "card_tier"]],
                   columns=["merchant_category", "channel", "card_tier"], drop_first=True, dtype=int)
X["log_amount"] = np.log1p(df.amount_usd)
X["log_distance"] = np.log1p(df.distance_from_home_km)
X["is_night"] = (df.hour <= 5).astype(int)
y = df.is_fraud

train = df.timestamp < "2026-07-01"
X_tr, X_te, y_tr, y_te = X[train], X[~train], y[train], y[~train]
print(f"Train: {len(X_tr):,} rows ({y_tr.mean()*100:.2f}% fraud) | Test: {len(X_te):,} rows ({y_te.mean()*100:.2f}% fraud)")

lr = make_pipeline(StandardScaler(), LogisticRegression(max_iter=2000, class_weight="balanced")).fit(X_tr, y_tr)
gb = HistGradientBoostingClassifier(max_iter=300, learning_rate=0.06, max_leaf_nodes=31,
                                    l2_regularization=1.0, random_state=7).fit(X_tr, y_tr)

scores = {}
for name, m in [("Logistic regression", lr), ("Gradient boosting", gb)]:
    p = m.predict_proba(X_te)[:, 1]
    scores[name] = p
    print(f"{name:22s} ROC-AUC {roc_auc_score(y_te, p):.3f} | PR-AUC {average_precision_score(y_te, p):.3f}")
print(f"{'Random baseline':22s} PR-AUC {y_te.mean():.3f}")

5. Choosing a threshold by dollars, not accuracy¶

The review team can't inspect everything. For each review capacity (share of transactions flagged) we compute:

  • Fraud $ caught — dollar value of fraudulent transactions in the flagged set
  • Review cost — $8 per flagged transaction (about 5 minutes of fully loaded analyst time)
  • Customer friction — $5 per legitimate customer flagged (call-center load, churn risk)
  • Net savings = fraud $ caught − review cost − friction
In [9]:
p = scores["Gradient boosting"]
test = df.loc[~train, ["amount_usd", "is_fraud"]].assign(score=p).sort_values("score", ascending=False).reset_index(drop=True)
REVIEW_COST, FRICTION_COST = 8.0, 5.0
total_fraud_usd = test.loc[test.is_fraud == 1, "amount_usd"].sum()

rows = []
for pct in [0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7.5, 10, 15]:
    k = int(len(test) * pct / 100)
    top = test.head(k)
    caught_usd = top.loc[top.is_fraud == 1, "amount_usd"].sum()
    fp = int((top.is_fraud == 0).sum())
    rows.append({"review_pct": pct, "flagged": k,
                 "fraud_caught": int(top.is_fraud.sum()),
                 "recall_pct": top.is_fraud.sum() / test.is_fraud.sum() * 100,
                 "precision_pct": top.is_fraud.mean() * 100,
                 "fraud_usd_caught": caught_usd,
                 "usd_recall_pct": caught_usd / total_fraud_usd * 100,
                 "net_savings_usd": caught_usd - k * REVIEW_COST - fp * FRICTION_COST})
curve = pd.DataFrame(rows)
best = curve.loc[curve.net_savings_usd.idxmax()]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 3.8))

7. Recommendations¶

  1. Deploy the gradient-boosting model and review the top 4% of scored transactions. On the held-out quarter this catches ~61% of fraud cases and ~74% of fraud dollars for ~$93K net savings per quarter (~$370K annualized). Beyond 4%, extra review cost outweighs the extra fraud caught.
  2. Add step-up authentication (3-D Secure / OTP) for card-not-present transactions from new devices, the single highest-lift combination; it would have blunted the April–June attack wave.
  3. Tighten limits on gift cards and digital goods for accounts under 90 days old, where fraud rates run several times the baseline.
  4. Monitor monthly fraud rate by channel with an alert threshold so the next attack wave is caught in weeks, not months.
  5. Retrain quarterly and track PR-AUC on the latest month to detect drift.

Limitations: data is synthetic; costs per review and per false positive are assumptions to be replaced with the issuer's real figures; chargeback timing and label delay are not modeled.
